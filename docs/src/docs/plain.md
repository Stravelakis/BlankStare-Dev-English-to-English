## In plain English

BlankStare is a free add-on for Google Chrome. It opens a panel at the side of your browser. When you read something written for programmers, like an error message, a setup guide or a page of documentation, you select it, and the panel rewrites it in plain English or Greek.

It does not add notes on top of the original. It gives you a version you can read **instead of** the original.

### What you need

- Google Chrome (or Edge, Brave, or another browser built on Chrome).
- A free key from Groq, the company whose computers write the answers. It takes about three minutes to get one and costs nothing.

### How you use it

1. Open the panel with the toolbar button or **Alt + Shift + B**.
2. Select the confusing text on any web page.
3. Press the small brass button that appears, or right-click and choose **Explain with BlankStare**.
4. Read the answer as it appears.

From there you can:

- **Change the reading level**, from "explain it to a child" up to "I build things with AI tools", and ask again.
- **Go deeper** for more detail, or **Rephrase** for a different wording.
- **See the jargon list**: every technical word, with a short meaning.
- **Hear it read aloud**, in English or Greek.
- **Summarise the whole page** in a few points.
- **Ask your own question** about what you selected.
- Look through your **last 20 answers**.

### Your privacy

- The text you select goes to Groq to be rewritten, or the whole page if you ask for a summary.
- Video and web searches go to YouTube or your search engine, only when you use them.
- Your keys are kept in your own browser.
- If you use your own search server, Chrome asks you to allow that one address, once. Say no and BlankStare uses DuckDuckGo instead.
- You can switch BlankStare off completely for any website you choose.

### Cost

Nothing. Groq's free allowance covers normal use. If one of its models is busy, BlankStare can move to the next one by itself.
